# Curiosity loop (per discoverable memory)

1. **Glimpse** — a corner, a shadow, a half-visible note. Never a "click here!" label.
2. **Invite** (optional) — after ~6–8 s idle, a tiny nudge; stop after twice.
3. **Gesture** — a physical metaphor she already knows: peel, lift, drag, trace, rub, unfold. Plain tap works too.
4. **Payoff** — the memory itself: *her* photo/words/voice. This is where the one big moment may live (rare → theatrical).
5. **Residue** — the page remembers (note stays moved, small "found" mark, a margin scribble appears). Persisted locally.
6. **Return** — a reason to come back (changes with date, unlocks, a new scribble after a visit).

## Checklist per memory
- [ ] Has real, specific content (not placeholder)? Would only she understand/enjoy the reference?
- [ ] Gesture has tap/keyboard fallback and works one-handed on a phone?
- [ ] Reduced-motion version keeps the payoff?
- [ ] Is it *optional* to discover (nothing essential hidden)?
- [ ] How long does it take on the 5th visit? (Repeat cost should be near zero or skippable.)

Theory: Norman's visceral/behavioral/reflective levels; Walter's hierarchy (pleasure sits above usability); Impeccable `delight` ("delight thesis", "reward curiosity without hiding required functionality"); Emil's frequency table.
